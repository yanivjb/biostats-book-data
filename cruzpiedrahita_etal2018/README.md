Here we summarize all variables, from left to right, presented in the data spreadsheet for the manuscript entitled: “Life on the edge: a comparative study of eco-physiological adaptations of frogs to tropical semiarid environments”.

Species and Individual

This column includes the genus, species, and the sequential (arbitrary) numbering of each individual frog. We apologize for note making this 3 columns, but the user can easily edit the file to her/his own needs. Note, amphibian taxonomy is very dynamic. For updates to taxonomy, see:

http://research.amnh.org/vz/herpetology/amphibia/index.php

For example, as this paper was going through the final round of reviews, Hypsiboas was replaced with Boana.

Genus

The taxonomic genus is repeated here. 

Exact location

Some specimens were collected in a specific, named location. 

Municipality

Known as Municipio in Colombia and analogous to ‘county’ in the USA. 

Department

Known as Departamento in Colombia and analogous to ‘state’ in the USA. 

Country

Colombia, South America. 

Annual precipitation in mm

Environmental data were taken from the WorldClim data base version 1.4 ( based on the approximate GPS coordinates of the collection site. ‘mm’ is millimeters. 

Seasonality cv

Environmental data were taken from the WorldClim data base version 1.4 ( based on the approximate GPS coordinates of the collection site. ‘cv’ is coefficient of variation.  

Average temperature in Celsius

Environmental data were taken from the WorldClim data base version 1.4 ( based on the approximate GPS coordinates of the collection site. ‘Average’ refers to ‘mean annual’.   

Habitat

This variable classifies the ecosystem that species inhabit in nature. This is a categorical variable, and we classified species in four categories: Dry forest, savannah, wet forest, and finally generalist is the term referring to species that can be found in multiple distinct ecosystems. Categories are based partly on precipitation levels at the exact collection sites.

Ecological habit

This categorical variable refers to where frogs are found normally in nature. If frogs live in trees or bushes, we qualify them as arboreal frogs. For species living in soil, we qualify them as terrestrial species. Semi-fossorial species, such a species of the genus Leptodactylus, were classified as terrestrial as well.

Collect (Field) numbers

These are the collector tag numbers given to each specimen. ‘AJC’ is the field tag of Andrew J. Crawford, though most collections were done by the first author, Catalina Cruz. See publication for details on permits. Specimens are deposited in the Museo de Historia Natural ANDES of the Universidad de los Andes, Bogotá.

Evaporative Water Loss (EWL) 

EWL refers to the amount of water lost by frogs during a period of time. We measured the initial weight of the frog at 100% hydration (and after discharging the bladder) and then we introduced the frog into a wind tunnel with a controlled airflow at a speed of 1.3 cm s⁻1. For each individual, we calculated de EWL from the regression body mass against time (gr min⁻¹). EWL is the slope of the curve resulted by this regression.

Dorsal area 

This measure is the quantification of skin surface of the dorsal part of the frog. In order to have a direct measurement and avoid biased formulas, we remove the skin of each frog and measured the surface using computer imaging.

EWL agar models 

EWL refers to the amount of water loss by agar models of frogs during a period of time. We measured the initial weight of the model and then we introduced it into a wind tunnel with a controlled airflow at a speed of 1.3 cm s⁻1. For each species (not each individual) we measured 5 models, including a model based in the smallest individual, the biggest individual, and three average individuals randomly selected. We calculated the EWL from the regression body mass against time (gr min⁻¹). EWL is the slope of the curve resulted by this regression.

Photo

D means dorsal photos. V means ventral photos. Digital photos are in the communicating author’s lab, but we hope to upload all photographs to BoLDsystems.org

EWL corrected by area 

This measurement refers to EWL rates divided by dorsal surface of the frog.

EWL for models corrected by area 

This measurement refers to EWL rates of models divided by dorsal surface of the frog they represent.

EWL phylogenetic residuals

This measurement refers to the residuals of each individual and the models, after being corrected by a phylogenetic size correction. Unit are in gr/(s*cm^2). Some values are positive and others are negative, this trend will depend on whether the value is below or above the slope. See manuscript for details.

Water Uptake (WU)

WU refers to the amount of water absorb by frogs from a liquid source during a period of time. For each individual, we calculated de WU from the regression body mass against time (gr min⁻¹). WU is the slope of the curve resulted by this regression.

Water Uptake from Moist paper Towel (WUMT) 

WUMT refers to the amount of water absorb by frogs from a wet paper towel during a period of time. For each individual, we calculated de WUMT from the regression body mass against time (gr min⁻¹). WU is the slope of the curve resulted by this regression.

Ventral area 

This measure is the quantification of skin surface of the ventral part of the frog. In order to have a direct measurement and avoid potentially biased formulas, we measured the surface directly using photography and computer analysis. See manuscript for details.

WU corrected by ventral area

This measurement refers to WU rates divided by ventral surface of the frog.

WUMT corrected by ventral area 

This measurement refers to WUMT rates divided by ventral surface of the frog.

Residuals of WU (gr/(s*cm^2))

This measurement refers to the residuals of WU for each individual, after being corrected by a phylogenetic size correction. Some values are positive and others are negative, this trend will depend if the value is under or above the general slope. This measurement was used to perform the comparison between species and congeneric populations.

Residuals of WUMT (gr/(s*cm^2))

This measurement refers to the residuals of WUMT for each individual, after being corrected by a phylogenetic size correction. Some values are positive and others are negative, this trend will depend if the value is under or above the general slope. This measurement was used to perform the comparison between species and congeneric populations.

Residuals DWU (gr/(s*cm^2))

This measurement refers to the residuals of DWU for each individual, after being corrected by a phylogenetic size correction. DWU refers to the difference between WU and WUMT. Some values are positive and others are negative, this trend will depend if the value is under or above the general slope. See text for more details.

Number of arms visited before reaching water

This variable quantifies the numbers of arms visited by each individual before it found the paper towel with water.

Time before reaching water

This variable quantifies the time it took to each individual to find the paper towel inside the radial enclosure. See text for details..

Body mass

This variable refers to the initial weight (in grams) each frog had at a 100% hydration state but after discharging excess water from the bladder.
